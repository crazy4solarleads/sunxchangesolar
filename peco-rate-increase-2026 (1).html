PECO Electricity Rates Are Rising Again: What Pennsylvania Homeowners Need to Know About Solar in 2026

Understand the December rate increase, Pennsylvania solar incentives, net metering and ways to take control of your energy costs.

SunXchange Energy Resources · Published and reviewed November 24, 2026

If you live in Philadelphia or southeastern Pennsylvania, your electric bill may be getting more expensive even if your usage stays the same. On December 1, 2026, PECO's default electricity supply benchmark is scheduled to rise again. Here is what that means for your household and how rooftop solar could change the way you buy electricity.

That is about 17.6% higher than December 2025. At 1,000 kWh per month, the December change alone adds $12.06 to the supply portion of the bill if usage stays constant. Delivery charges and other fees are separate.

Why are rates rising?

PECO is part of the PJM regional electricity market. Growing electricity demand, including large new loads such as data centers, and insufficient new generation can increase wholesale supply and capacity costs. PECO cited the regional supply-demand imbalance in its November announcement. [2] Weather and household usage also influence the amount you actually pay.

Understanding your PECO electricity bill

Your bill includes electricity supply, delivery charges, recurring customer charges and applicable adjustments or taxes. Divide your electricity-related bill by the kWh used to estimate your average effective cost. For example, $240 divided by 1,000 kWh equals 24¢/kWh. This is an illustration, not a PECO tariff.

For a solar comparison, separate fixed charges from variable charges that solar can offset. A grid-connected solar home generally continues to receive a utility bill. You can also compare competitive electricity suppliers, but review contract terms and net metering treatment before switching.

How does PECO net metering work?

Solar panels generate electricity in daylight. Your home consumes available solar power first; excess generation can flow to the grid after interconnection approval. At night or during low production, the home imports electricity. Net metering accounts for these flows.

Under Pennsylvania rules, eligible residential systems up to 50 kW can participate, subject to requirements. For default-service customers, eligible monthly net energy offsets generally include retail generation, transmission and distribution components. Surplus credits roll forward within the annual cycle; remaining credits at annual settlement are generally valued at the Price to Compare, not the full retail rate. Competitive suppliers may treat net metering differently. [4] [5]

Pennsylvania solar incentives in 2026

Solar renewable energy credits (SRECs)

Qualifying registered solar projects can earn tradable solar alternative energy credits under Pennsylvania's AEPS framework. One credit represents 1,000 kWh of qualifying generation. A system generating 10,000 kWh per year could earn about ten credits. Confirm who owns the credits in your contract; third-party ownership can change who receives the proceeds. [5]

Net metering

Net metering can reduce the cost of electricity bought from the grid throughout the life of an eligible system, subject to applicable rules. It is not an upfront cash rebate.

Federal residential solar tax credit

The former 30% homeowner Residential Clean Energy Credit generally does not apply to installations completed after December 31, 2025. Separate business incentives may be available to qualifying third-party owners. Consult a tax professional for your circumstances. [6]

Going solar with $0 down in PECO territory

A power purchase agreement (PPA) lets a third-party provider own the solar system while the homeowner pays for its electricity under contract. Qualifying programs may include no upfront installation payment, monitoring and maintenance under the agreement. Review the term, escalator, production assumptions, buyout provisions and home sale conditions.

Can solar power your home during a PECO outage?

Ordinary grid-tied solar shuts down during a grid outage for safety. Compatible batteries and islanding equipment can keep selected loads powered, and properly configured systems may recharge from solar in daylight. Backup duration depends on available storage, weather and consumption.

Is solar right for your property?

Homeowners across Philadelphia, Bucks, Chester, Delaware and Montgomery counties can explore solar where PECO service and installation eligibility apply. Roof condition, shading, available space, system production and contract terms determine whether the economics make sense. Review a full year of bills before sizing a system.

Frequently asked questions

Is PECO raising rates in December 2026?

Yes. The default residential supply benchmark is scheduled to increase to 12.965¢/kWh on December 1.

Does PECO offer net metering?

Eligible grid-connected systems can participate subject to Pennsylvania rules and utility approval.

Can I eliminate my PECO bill?

Solar may offset variable energy charges, but fixed charges and any uncovered usage generally remain.

Can I sell excess solar power?

Eligible surplus is credited under net metering rules, with remaining annual credits generally settled at the applicable supply benchmark.

Is $0-down solar free?

No. A PPA or lease may require no upfront installation payment, but ongoing contractual payments apply.
